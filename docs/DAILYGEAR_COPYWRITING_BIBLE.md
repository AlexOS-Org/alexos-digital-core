# DailyGear SEO & Conversion Copywriting Bible

## Purpose

DailyGear copy must do two jobs at once: help a shopper find the right product and make the next action feel easy, credible and worthwhile.

The rule is **persuasion without fabrication**. We may sharpen benefits, clarify use cases, reduce friction and create stronger calls to action, but we must not invent specifications, stock, reviews, guarantees, discounts, delivery promises or product performance claims that are not supported by catalogue evidence.

## 1. Product title formula

Use:

**[Brand/Model] + [Product Type] + [important differentiator]**

Examples:

- `NAVIFORCE Ladies Watch #NF5060`
- `YJ Children’s School Backpack`
- `30,000mAh Solar Wireless Power Bank`
- `3 Column Plastic Wardrobe`

Avoid:

- social-media captions as titles
- emojis at the beginning of titles
- ALL CAPS
- claims such as “BEST”, “OFFICIAL”, “UNBREAKABLE”, “LUXURY” unless independently supported
- price or discount language inside the product name
- unfinished sentences copied from an advert

Keep titles concise, descriptive and search-aligned. The product name should identify what the shopper is actually buying.

## 2. Short description formula

Use one sentence that answers:

**What is it + who is it for + useful benefit + important option/specification.**

Target roughly 80–155 characters where the available evidence allows it. Longer copy is acceptable when a key buying detail needs to be retained.

Example:

> Water-resistant YJ children’s school backpack with organised storage, padded support and colour/size options for everyday school carry.

Do not turn the short description into a feature dump. Lead with the customer outcome, then add the evidence that reduces uncertainty.

## 3. Full product description structure

1. **Outcome-led opening** — state the practical problem or desired outcome.
2. **Key benefits** — 3–5 scannable benefits using only verified facts.
3. **Specifications** — dimensions, material, capacity, sizes, colours, compatibility, etc.
4. **What to choose** — make variants obvious.
5. **Availability note** — only use current availability data.
6. **Delivery/payment note** — never promise what the checkout cannot actually provide.
7. **CTA** — direct and specific.

## 4. Persuasion frameworks

### AIDA

- **Attention:** make the first line relevant to the buyer’s problem.
- **Interest:** explain the useful difference.
- **Desire:** connect verified features to an everyday outcome.
- **Action:** give one obvious next step.

### PAS

Use carefully for problem-solving products:

- **Problem:** name the friction the shopper recognises.
- **Agitation:** show why the friction matters without fearmongering.
- **Solution:** position the product as the practical next step.

### Benefit → proof → action

Every important claim should follow this pattern when possible:

> **Benefit:** Keep school essentials organised.  
> **Proof:** The catalogue lists multiple compartments and a dedicated device compartment.  
> **Action:** Choose the available colour and size.

### Objection handling

Address the buying questions that block action:

- What exactly am I getting?
- Which size/colour should I choose?
- Is it available?
- How much will delivery cost?
- How do I pay?
- Can I order without creating an account?
- What happens after I order?

## 5. Trust rules

Never manufacture:

- customer reviews
- star ratings
- scarcity such as “only 2 left” unless stock is genuinely current
- “limited time” urgency without a real campaign end
- warranty terms not stored in the catalogue
- delivery times not supported by operations
- “free delivery” unless the configured shipping rules actually make it free
- “official”, “genuine”, “original” or “authentic” unless the business has evidence

Where evidence is historical, the copy must not present it as current proof of price or availability.

## 6. SEO rules

Every indexable product should have:

- unique descriptive title
- unique meta description
- clean slug
- useful visible product text
- accurate image alt text
- Product/Offer structured data
- availability and price consistent between page, structured data and catalogue
- canonical URL
- breadcrumb navigation
- internal links from category pages to products

Google specifically recommends informative product descriptions matching shopper search language, descriptive page titles and meta descriptions, and ecommerce structured data. Product structured data can expose price, availability, shipping and return information where eligible.

## 7. CTA rules

Prefer specific action verbs:

- `Choose your size`
- `Choose a colour`
- `Add to cart`
- `Buy now`
- `Review and order`
- `Continue to checkout`

Avoid vague CTAs such as `Submit`, `Proceed`, `Learn More` or `Click Here` when a more informative action is possible.

## 8. Storefront voice

DailyGear should sound:

- practical
- confident
- helpful
- concise
- Kenyan-market aware
- commercially direct without sounding desperate

The storefront should answer the shopper’s question quickly: **“Is this useful to me, can I trust the offer, and what do I do next?”**

## 9. Email copy system

### Order confirmation

Subject should confirm the outcome first:

> Order [number] received — your DailyGear order is on its way to confirmation

Body sequence:

1. Thank the customer.
2. Confirm the order reference.
3. Summarise the basket and total.
4. State the next operational step.
5. Give payment instructions only when required.
6. Explain how to get help.

### Abandoned cart

Use a single clear reminder rather than pressure:

1. Personal greeting.
2. Remind them the basket is saved.
3. Show the selected products and subtotal.
4. Reinforce one or two legitimate reasons to finish.
5. Give one recovery CTA.
6. Give an easy support route.
7. Allow the customer to ignore the message without guilt.

### Promotional email

Recommended structure:

**Hook → benefit → proof → offer → urgency (only if real) → CTA → reassurance.**

Promotional claims must always be tied to a real campaign, price or stock condition.

## 10. Kenya-specific checkout copy

Keep location selection explicit and familiar:

- Country
- County
- Town
- Delivery details
- Phone number

Do not force account creation to complete a purchase. Ask only for information needed to fulfil and support the order.

## 11. Final copy QA checklist

Before publishing any copy:

- Is the product immediately identifiable?
- Does the first sentence explain why the product matters?
- Are the claims supported by catalogue evidence?
- Is the copy easy to scan on a phone?
- Does the title contain the terms a real shopper would search?
- Is the CTA a clear action?
- Are price, stock, variants and delivery claims current?
- Does the copy reduce a real purchase objection?
- Does the page contain enough visible text for search engines to understand the product?
- Is the same promise consistent across product page, funnel, checkout and email?

## Source principles

This playbook is informed by Google Search Central ecommerce guidance and established conversion-copy principles including AIDA, PAS, benefit-led copy, objection handling and friction reduction. It is an internal operating standard, not a substitute for factual product evidence.
